# Scenario #001: Audible Beeping During Transitioning

## Scenario Overview

This scenario documents Kevin Mahan's report of audible beeping associated with movement inside a parked vehicle at approximately 04:25 CDT on October 8, 2026, and a prior report of similar beeping upon awakening. The sound source was not independently identified in the initial observation. The perceived relationship to transitioning is an investigative question, not an established cause or finding.

## Transitioning — Operational Definition

For this scenario, **transitioning** means a change between physical activities or states of movement. It includes:

1. Awakening from sleep.
2. Beginning movement after resting.
3. Moving inside a parked vehicle.
4. Unlocking a vehicle.
5. Opening or closing vehicle doors.
6. Activating vehicle electrical systems.
7. Beginning ordinary activities after an extended stationary period.

This scenario uses the term only in this operational sense, not as a substitute for an unrelated psychological or medical definition.

## Investigative Hypotheses

These hypotheses are separate, unverified possibilities. Each requires evidence that supports or contradicts it.

### Hypothesis A — Behavioral Conditioning

It is hypothesized that recurring audible signals associated with predictable transitions may contribute to a learned anticipatory response. Repeated exposure to a recognizable sound during awakening or movement could create an association between the activity and the audible stimulus.

Potential responses to document, if actually experienced, include increased anticipation, heightened attention, irritation, psychological discomfort, disrupted concentration, or stress associated with transitioning.

Intentional behavioral conditioning is one proposed explanation. Its occurrence, mechanism, and intentionality have not been established by the initial observation.

### Hypothesis B — Visual Line-of-Sight Correlation

Movement inside a vehicle may be externally visible under some lighting conditions. Relevant factors may include window visibility, interior and exterior lighting, vehicle position, and environmental illumination. Investigate whether observable changes occur around the same time as the sound. A correlation must not be treated as proof of monitoring or intentional signaling.

### Hypothesis C — Vehicle Electronic Activity

Vehicle systems may produce audible signals during ordinary operation. Possible sources to evaluate include keyless entry systems, vehicle alarms, proximity sensors, door-locking mechanisms, electronic notifications, and nearby equipment. Technical inspection or testing may help assess these possibilities; no such inspection is reported as completed.

### Hypothesis D — Other Environmental Sources

Other possible explanations include nearby electronic equipment, equipment alarms, parking-area electronics, environmental sound sources, or unrelated coincidental sounds. Evaluate each separately and record evidence for and against it rather than inferring a source from proximity alone.

## Investigative Questions

- Does a repeatable relationship exist between transitioning and the occurrence of the sound?
- Can another observer independently detect the sound at the reported time?
- Can synchronized recordings establish the sound's timing and characteristics?
- Can a physical sound source be identified, rather than merely an object or vehicle present nearby?
- Do vehicle electronic activity, visible environmental conditions, or other ambient sources correspond with the event?
- Which observations or evidence support or contradict each hypothesis?
- Does pre-transition music change perceived disruption or reported discomfort, without being treated as evidence about the sound's source?
