# Research Objectives

The project aims to make reported experiences and relevant technical questions more systematically describable and independently reviewable. The objectives below are goals; they do not assert that a measurement, experiment, or verification has already occurred.

## Objectives

1. **Document observations.** Preserve reported experiences and instrument records in their original context, separating the observation from later interpretation.
2. **Identify measurable phenomena.** Define what could be recorded or measured, such as timing, waveform characteristics, environmental conditions, device settings, or independent detection.
3. **Evaluate competing hypotheses.** Compare conventional acoustic sources, electronic interference, recording and playback artifacts, auditory pattern recognition, and other testable explanations without selecting a cause from resemblance alone.
4. **Conduct historical research.** Locate and assess primary records relevant to technologies or research programs, recording provenance and context.
5. **Conduct technical research.** Review relevant scientific and engineering publications and distinguish established findings from hypotheses, patent claims, and personal reports.
6. **Preserve evidence.** Protect originals, record provenance and transformations, and maintain traceable links among evidence, observations, and analysis.
7. **Support independent verification.** Make methods and relevant records available for qualified, independent examination where appropriate and lawful.
8. **Assess reproducibility.** Define procedures and criteria in advance, record null and conflicting results, and determine whether an observation recurs under documented conditions.
9. **Enable legal and scientific review.** Preserve the distinctions among experience, instrument capture, technical interpretation, source identification, and evidence of intent so appropriate professionals can assess the material.

## Evidence Status

Progress toward an objective must be described only when supporting records exist. A planned method is not a completed experiment; a personal report is not independent corroboration; and a technical possibility is not a verified explanation of an incident.
