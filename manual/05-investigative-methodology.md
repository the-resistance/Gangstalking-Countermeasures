# Investigative Methodology

The procedures in this chapter are proposed practices for producing reviewable records. They are not claims that an investigation or measurement has already been performed. Follow applicable law, protect privacy, and do not put people at risk to collect data.

## Before Recording

1. State the question being investigated and list plausible alternative explanations before collecting data.
2. Define what will be observed, the observation period, comparison conditions, and what outcome would support or weaken each hypothesis.
3. Record the date, local time and time zone, general location, relevant environmental conditions, nearby equipment, and any known limitations. Use the repository's missing-information designations rather than estimating unknown facts.
4. Do not change several conditions at once. If a change is necessary, record what changed and when.

## Environmental Baseline

Record a baseline period before a relevant activity or equipment change, using the same location and recording setup planned for the comparison where practical. Note equipment that is actually operating, nearby activity, and relevant environmental conditions. If a safe and authorized comparison period with equipment off is possible, record it separately; do not interfere with equipment or assume that a difference establishes causation.

## Recording Equipment and Settings

For each device, record what is available:

- Manufacturer, model, serial or asset identifier if appropriate, and operating system.
- Recording application and version.
- Microphone type, placement, orientation, and any external accessories.
- Gain, input level, noise reduction, automatic gain control, or other enabled processing.
- Sample rate, bit depth if available, channel count, file format, and original filename.
- Start and stop times, clock reference, and any known clock offset.
- Playback device and settings when playback is part of the observation.

Do not infer settings that the device does not expose. If a setting is unknown, record it as unknown. Do not alter the only original recording to normalize, filter, amplify, or convert it.

## Independent Devices and Environmental Controls

When lawful, safe, and practical, use independently operating recording devices and document their positions and settings. Avoid relying on devices that share the same microphone, signal path, or automatic processing when the purpose is independent capture. Record periods when no event is perceived as well as periods when one is reported. Keep device placement and settings consistent for comparison sessions, or document each deviation.

## Audio Review and Spectrum Analysis

Analyze copies, not originals. Record the tool and software version, processing steps, parameters, and output filenames. Spectrum analysis may describe signal characteristics, but by itself it cannot identify a speaker, source, intent, or cause. Preserve unprocessed material alongside every derivative, and distinguish measured features from interpretations.

## Blind Listening Comparisons

Where appropriate, ask multiple listeners to assess the same unaltered recording independently before telling them what words, speakers, or event they are expected to hear. Preserve each listener's original account, confidence or uncertainty, playback conditions, and any later discussion separately. If comparing processed and original audio, document versions and present them in a manner that limits expectation effects. Listener agreement is not, by itself, proof of source or identity.

## Alternative-Hypothesis Testing

For each candidate explanation, state what evidence would be expected if it were correct and what evidence could count against it. Consider conventional environmental sound, equipment operation, electronic interference, recording and playback artifacts, and ambiguous sound perception where relevant. Do not treat temporal association, proximity, or resemblance as proof of causation or attribution.

## Repeatability and Source Identification

Repeat a procedure only when safe, lawful, and practical. Record every session, including null results and changes in conditions. Use predefined criteria and report inconsistent results. Source identification requires evidence that distinguishes the source from other plausible sources; a nearby object, device, person, or vehicle alone does not establish that it produced the sound.

## Reporting Results

Separate the report into:

1. Reported experience and contemporaneous notes.
2. Instrument capture and acquisition details.
3. Processing and measurements.
4. Independent observations or analyses.
5. Alternative explanations and limitations.
6. Results that remain unresolved.

Do not claim a measurement, independent confirmation, or repeatability unless its supporting records are available.
