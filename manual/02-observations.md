# Observations

This chapter is an index of reported observation categories in the existing research record, not a new incident record. The descriptions below reflect [RSH-001, “Personal Field Observations”](../research/001-voice-to-skull-v2k.md#5-personal-field-observations). RSH-001 states that the underlying causes have not been independently established. No new dates, locations, measurements, recordings, or witnesses are supplied here.

## Observation Versus Explanation

An observation is a report of what a person perceived or a device recorded. Terms such as “synthetic,” “responsive,” “impersonation,” or “interference” can suggest mechanisms; they must remain hypotheses unless independently supported. A sound that resembles speech does not by itself establish intelligible speech, a speaker, a transmission method, or intent.

For each future record, preserve the original description and distinguish:

- what the observer reported;
- what an instrument captured, if an original recording exists;
- what independent listeners or instruments detected;
- what hypotheses and alternatives were considered; and
- what, if anything, was independently verified.

Do not treat this summary as a replacement for the source document. The original account and its qualifications remain in [RSH-001](../research/001-voice-to-skull-v2k.md). The separately documented [SCN-001 — Audible Beeping During Transitioning](../scenarios/001-audible-beeping-transitioning.md) remains an independent scenario record, with its own protected original observation, limitations, and unresolved status.
