# Terminology Registry

The terms in this registry are project-specific working classifications drawn from [RSH-001, “Proposed Terminology and Working Hypotheses”](../research/001-voice-to-skull-v2k.md#4-proposed-terminology-and-working-hypotheses). They are not established scientific classifications, diagnoses, verified mechanisms, or evidence that a technology was used. They provide consistent labels for recording questions that remain open.

For every entry, “verification criteria” describe evidence that would be relevant to evaluating the question; they do not imply that the criteria have been met. No independent evidence record or verification result is identified for these classifications in the existing research document.

## SCS — Synthetic Conversational Simulation

- **Full terminology:** Synthetic Conversational Simulation.
- **Definition:** A proposed model in which computationally generated voices appear to participate in a continuous conversation.
- **Associated reported observation:** Speech-like sounds were reported as sometimes seeming to involve several parties taking turns or responding.
- **Proposed technical explanations:** Externally generated synthetic dialogue is one hypothesis; the research record does not identify a generating system or transmission path.
- **Alternative explanations:** Conventional acoustic sources, ambiguous noise interpreted as speech, recordings, and recording or playback artifacts remain alternatives.
- **Required measurements:** Preserve original audio if available; document device and settings; assess intelligibility, speaker changes, event timing, independent listener interpretations, and capture on independent devices.
- **Verification criteria:** Predefined blind comparisons should show repeatable agreement about speech and speaker changes, with independent capture or other source evidence before attributing the sound to a system.
- **Current evidence status:** Personal report in RSH-001; no independent confirmation, recording, or identified source is documented there.
- **Related research references:** [RSH-001 §4.1](../research/001-voice-to-skull-v2k.md#41-synthetic-conversational-simulation-scs), [observations chapter](02-observations.md), and [technology research](04-technology-research.md).

## CRS — Conversational Role Simulation

- **Full terminology:** Conversational Role Simulation.
- **Definition:** A proposed component of SCS in which apparent voices seem to represent different personalities, characters, or social roles.
- **Associated reported observation:** Different apparent speakers were reported as seeming to participate in coordinated conversation.
- **Proposed technical explanations:** Scripted or generated dialogue is a hypothesis; no particular software or external source is established.
- **Alternative explanations:** Multiple ordinary sound sources, ambiguous environmental sound, expectation, and recording artifacts remain alternatives.
- **Required measurements:** Record original audio if available; obtain independent, blinded descriptions of speaker count, turn-taking, word content, and timing.
- **Verification criteria:** Independent listeners should demonstrate repeatable, predefined agreement about distinct speakers and turns; source attribution requires additional evidence.
- **Current evidence status:** Personal report in RSH-001; the existence of distinct speakers and their source remain unverified.
- **Related research references:** [RSH-001 §4.2](../research/001-voice-to-skull-v2k.md#42-conversational-role-simulation-crs), [SCS](#scs--synthetic-conversational-simulation), and [technology research](04-technology-research.md).

## SVIM — Synthetic Vocal Identity Misattribution

- **Full terminology:** Synthetic Vocal Identity Misattribution.
- **Definition:** A working label for perceived or recorded speech that seems to resemble a recognizable individual when the actual source has not been established.
- **Associated reported observation:** Some sounds were reported as resembling voices of people personally familiar to the observer.
- **Proposed technical explanations:** Synthetic voice imitation is one hypothesis; no synthetic source is identified in the record.
- **Alternative explanations:** Ambiguous audio, ordinary speakers, recording effects, and familiarity-driven interpretation remain alternatives.
- **Required measurements:** Preserve source audio; conduct blinded listener comparisons; document intelligibility and acoustic characteristics; separately seek evidence identifying the source.
- **Verification criteria:** Voice resemblance and speaker identity must be assessed separately; identity attribution requires independent, source-specific evidence, not familiarity alone.
- **Current evidence status:** Personal report in RSH-001; no speaker identity or synthetic origin has been verified.
- **Related research references:** [RSH-001 §4.3](../research/001-voice-to-skull-v2k.md#43-synthetic-vocal-identity-misattribution-svim), [observations chapter](02-observations.md), and [technology research](04-technology-research.md).

## PCAR — Perceived Contextual Audio Responsiveness

- **Full terminology:** Perceived Contextual Audio Responsiveness.
- **Definition:** An observation category for sounds that seem temporally related to the observer's actions, activities, or surroundings.
- **Associated reported observation:** Some sounds were reported as seeming connected in time to nearby activities.
- **Proposed technical explanations:** A repeatable relationship to an independently recorded event is a hypothesis to test, not a finding.
- **Alternative explanations:** Coincidence, routine environmental sound, selective attention, and incomplete or inaccurate timing remain alternatives.
- **Required measurements:** Record audio and the relevant activity on independent time references; document clock offsets, exposure periods, comparison periods, and null events.
- **Verification criteria:** A pre-specified temporal association should recur across documented observations and be independently assessed against comparison conditions; correlation alone would not establish cause or intent.
- **Current evidence status:** Personal report in RSH-001; no synchronized measurements or controlled relationship is documented.
- **Related research references:** [RSH-001 §4.4](../research/001-voice-to-skull-v2k.md#44-perceived-contextual-audio-responsiveness-pcar), [investigative methodology](05-investigative-methodology.md), and [SCN-001](../scenarios/001-audible-beeping-transitioning.md).

## EAC — Environmental Audio Coupling

- **Full terminology:** Environmental Audio Coupling.
- **Definition:** A working category for investigating an apparent relationship between environmental equipment and perceived or recorded audio.
- **Associated reported observation:** Speech-like sounds were reported in environments with motors, ventilation, or air-conditioning equipment.
- **Proposed technical explanations:** Mechanical vibration, acoustic resonance, electrical or electromagnetic interference, background-noise modulation, and recording artifacts are possibilities listed in RSH-001.
- **Alternative explanations:** Independent environmental sound sources, unrelated coincidence, and perceptual interpretation remain alternatives.
- **Required measurements:** Document equipment state and environment; compare controlled operating and non-operating periods when safe; use independent recordings and record device settings.
- **Verification criteria:** Any claimed relationship should be reproducible under documented comparison conditions and supported by measurements that distinguish the equipment from other sources.
- **Current evidence status:** Personal report and candidate mechanisms in RSH-001; no equipment source or coupling has been independently demonstrated in the repository.
- **Related research references:** [RSH-001 §4.5](../research/001-voice-to-skull-v2k.md#45-environmental-audio-coupling-eac), [observations chapter](02-observations.md), and [technology research](04-technology-research.md).

## ESPF — Environmental Speech-like Pattern Formation

- **Full terminology:** Environmental Speech-like Pattern Formation.
- **Definition:** A working label for perceiving speech-like patterns in environmental noise.
- **Associated reported observation:** Environmental sounds were reported as sometimes seeming speech-like.
- **Proposed technical explanations:** Whether a recognizable speech signal is present in the waveform is an open measurement question.
- **Alternative explanations:** Ambiguous noise interpreted as speech, conventional acoustic speech, and recording or playback artifacts remain alternatives.
- **Required measurements:** Preserve unaltered audio when available; collect independent, blinded listener descriptions and compare them with the waveform and environmental baseline.
- **Verification criteria:** Predefined, independent listening results should be repeatable; the label alone does not establish that intelligible speech or a speaker is present.
- **Current evidence status:** Working classification based on reported perception; no independent speech identification is documented in RSH-001.
- **Related research references:** [RSH-001 §4.6](../research/001-voice-to-skull-v2k.md#46-environmental-speech-like-pattern-formation-espf), [observations chapter](02-observations.md), and [technology research](04-technology-research.md).

## HGVA — High-Gain Vocal-like Artifacts

- **Full terminology:** High-Gain Vocal-like Artifacts.
- **Definition:** A working label for speech-like sounds noticed after microphone gain, playback volume, or digital amplification is increased.
- **Associated reported observation:** Faint vocal-like sounds were reported during amplified recording playback.
- **Proposed technical explanations:** Amplification may reveal weak recorded material or emphasize electronic, processing, or background-noise characteristics; the cause is untested.
- **Alternative explanations:** Perceptual interpretation, ordinary sound, compression, automatic gain control, and other playback differences remain alternatives.
- **Required measurements:** Preserve original and derivative files; document gain and playback settings; compare normal-gain and amplified versions through blinded listening and file-integrity checks.
- **Verification criteria:** The signal and its transformation should be reproducible from preserved files and documented settings; amplification alone does not establish a voice or its source.
- **Current evidence status:** Personal report in RSH-001; no recording, settings, or independent analysis is identified there.
- **Related research references:** [RSH-001 §4.7](../research/001-voice-to-skull-v2k.md#47-high-gain-vocal-like-artifacts-hgva), [investigative methodology](05-investigative-methodology.md), and [evidence preservation](06-evidence-preservation.md).
