# CM-002 — Pre-Transition Auditory Masking and Response Interruption

This proposed procedure is associated with [SCN-001](../scenarios/001-audible-beeping-transitioning.md). Follow the [Versioning Policy](../governance/VERSIONING.md) and [Anti-Drift Policy](../governance/ANTI_DRIFT.md). It evaluates the listener's reported experience; it does not establish or change an external sound's source or occurrence.

## Objective

Investigate whether listening to familiar music before and during an appropriate, stationary transition is associated with reduced perceived disruption, reported discomfort, or concentration difficulty. Compare measurable observations across separate transitions without assuming an external cause or proven effectiveness.

## Applicable Conditions

Use only when the participant is stationary and does not need to hear traffic, vehicle, environmental, or other safety warnings. Do not use headphones or earbuds while driving, operating a vehicle, crossing roads, or doing any activity that requires environmental awareness. For awakening-related observations, playback may begin before getting up only when it is safe and practical.

## Technical Basis

Audio playback may mask some external sounds at the listener's ear and may redirect attention. This can change perception or reported experience without changing whether an external sound occurred or what produced it. Individual hearing, fit, device output, ambient sound, and volume affect masking. The proposed effects—including reducing anticipatory discomfort or interrupting a possible learned association—are hypotheses to evaluate, not established results.

## Required Equipment

- Headphones or earbuds, if appropriate and safe.
- A playback device and familiar music.
- A written incident log or the [SCN-001 incident recording form](../scenarios/001-audible-beeping-transitioning.md#incident-recording-template).

## Preparation

1. Choose a safe, stationary setting and a transition that does not require listening for warnings.
2. Select familiar music and a comfortable, moderate playback volume that does not cause discomfort.
3. If comparing conditions, plan in advance to record separate transitions with and without playback. Do not change other conditions intentionally in a way that creates a safety risk.
4. Record the planned condition and relevant context. Do not treat a perceived change as evidence about a sound's source.

## Procedure

1. **Condition A — no playback:** During an appropriate, safe transition, do not use headphones or music. Record the transition time, whether an audible event was perceived, reported discomfort and concentration using the same chosen scale, and relevant context.
2. **Condition B — playback:** For a separate appropriate transition, begin familiar music before movement. Record headphone type, music start time, transition time, and a consistent description of playback volume.
3. Continue playback only while stationary and when safety allows. Stop playback if an environmental or vehicle warning must be heard, or if discomfort occurs.
4. Record whether an audible event was perceived, reported discomfort, attention or concentration, event duration if known, and the total period observed. Distinguish not heard from not occurring; masking can limit perception.
5. Repeat across separate transitions only when safe and practical. Record each session, including outcomes where no difference is noticed. Avoid changing playback or recording methods without documenting the change.

## Expected Results

A comparison record may show whether perceived sound exposure, self-reported discomfort, or concentration differed between playback and no-playback conditions. No benefit or effect is guaranteed; the external event and source may be unchanged.

## Evidence Collection

Record headphone type, music start time, transition time, playback volume description, observation period, whether an event was perceived, and the participant's reported response. Preserve actual recordings only if they were collected; do not create fictional evidence to support a comparison. Follow the [Evidence Documentation Standards](../evidence/README.md) and [Evidence Log Template](../templates/EVIDENCE_LOG_TEMPLATE.md).

## Effectiveness Evaluation

Compare multiple safe, separate transitions with and without music using the same observation period and the same participant-report scale. Report the number of sessions and the actual recorded outcomes for perceived sound exposure, discomfort, concentration, and anticipatory response. Note differences in conditions and missing observations. Do not claim effectiveness from a single event or treat reduced perception as proof that the external sound stopped.

## Limitations

Auditory masking can prevent the listener from noticing important sounds and cannot identify or remove an external source. Self-reports are subjective; conditions may differ between transitions; the participant may know which condition is being used; and a small number of observations may not show a repeatable effect. No result establishes behavioral conditioning or intentional conduct.

## Safety Considerations

Never use headphones or earbuds while driving or operating a vehicle. Keep volume comfortable and low enough to avoid hearing discomfort. Use this procedure only while stationary and when environmental and vehicle warnings can still be heard; remove or pause headphones immediately if safety requires awareness.
